The component pages tell you how each piece works. These pages tell you which piece to pick. Most interface problems are not a broken component, they are the right component in the wrong place: a form crammed into a popover, a validation error shown as a toast, a menu of links pretending to be a menu of actions.

A library gives you parts. A design system also tells you how the parts fit together, so two people building two screens end up with the same answers. That is what this section is for.

## What you will find

- [Overlays](/docs/guidelines/overlays): Dialog, Alert Dialog, Sheet, Drawer, Popover, Hover Card and Tooltip. Which one blocks the page, which one needs an answer, and how much each can hold.
- [Feedback](/docs/guidelines/feedback): Toast, Banner, Callout, field errors, Alert Dialog and Empty, sorted by urgency, how long they stay and how much of the app they speak for.
- [Selection](/docs/guidelines/selection): Radio, Select, Combobox, Toggle Group, Checkbox, Switch, Slider and Number Field, by how many options there are and when the choice takes effect.
- [Navigation](/docs/guidelines/navigation): Tabs, Navigation Menu, Sidebar, Breadcrumb, Pagination, Menubar and Dropdown Menu, and the line between going somewhere and doing something.
- [Forms](/docs/guidelines/forms): labels, descriptions, errors, when to validate, where the buttons go and how to mark what is optional.
- [Writing](/docs/guidelines/writing): the voice of the system, button labels, error messages, empty states, dates and numbers.

## How to read them

Each page starts with a decision table. Read it as a first filter, not a law: find the row that matches what you are building and you are usually done.

Then come the examples. They are real NachUI components, not screenshots, so you can open the overlays, click the switches and submit the forms. Every pair has a verdict in words, not only in color:

Every rule comes with its reason. If a rule does not fit your case, the reason tells you whether you found an exception or a mistake. When you break one on purpose, write down why in the pull request so the next person does not have to guess.

## What they are not

They are not a list of what the components can do. A Popover can hold a ten field form and nothing in the code will stop you. The guidelines describe what works for the people using your product, which is a narrower set than what compiles.

They also do not replace testing with real people. If a pattern from these pages confuses your users, believe the users and tell us about it.
